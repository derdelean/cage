# Patent Pledge: Defensive-Only Enforcement

**Version 1.1, 21 October 2026** (version 1.0: 24 May 2026)

## Summary

CAGE is open-source under the [Apache License 2.0](./LICENSE). Its Section 3 already grants every user a perpetual, royalty-free patent license to all patent claims practiced by the licensed work.

This document extends that grant with a **binding, public, irrevocable pledge**: the patent rights described below will not be enforced offensively against good-faith users.

## The pledge

The following patent rights:

- the Swiss patent application filed on **21 October 2025**,
- the Swiss patent application filed on **19 September 2026**,
- any application in any jurisdiction claiming priority from either of them,
- any continuation, divisional or reissue of any of the above, and any patent granted on them,

will **not be enforced** against any individual, organization or project for any use that falls under one of the following:

1. **Research and education.** Academic research, teaching, student projects, published research papers, security analysis and reproducibility studies.
2. **Open-source and free software.** Any project distributed under an Open Source Initiative-approved license or a Free Software Foundation-recognized free software license.
3. **AI-safety research and tooling.** Any project whose primary purpose is improving the safety, alignment, interpretability, control or governance of AI systems.
4. **Defensive use.** Any party using the patented technology to protect its own systems, customers or research infrastructure, including commercial defensive use.
5. **Non-commercial use.** Any individual or non-profit using the technology without direct commercial monetization.

## What is not covered

This pledge does **not** waive enforcement against a party who:

- files offensive patent litigation against the patent holder, the CAGE project, or any contributor or user,
- patents CAGE-derived or substantially similar inventions and asserts those patents against third parties, or
- misrepresents the origin of the technology, or strips the patent attribution from derivative works, in a way intended to harm the patent holder or the public commons.

In such cases the pledge is suspended for the offending party, and only for that party. The patent holder keeps the right to defend the commons and themselves.

## Why a pledge in addition to Apache 2.0

Apache 2.0 Section 3 grants a patent license but says little in public about intent. A standalone pledge:

- tells the AI-safety, academic and open-source communities, who often face patent uncertainty, what they can rely on,
- creates a public commitment on record, comparable to Tesla's 2014 EV patent pledge and Twitter's Innovator's Patent Agreement, and
- matches the project's purpose: cryptographic infrastructure that keeps humans in control of the AI systems they deploy.
